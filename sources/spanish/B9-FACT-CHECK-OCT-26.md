## Points that rest on one source, on an inference, or on the course's own judgment (for B14)

1. *así como* glossed "as soon as" (the Ormsby rendering and general usage; not checked in the DLE entry *así*).
2. *quitar la vida* "to kill", *lugar* "a small place", *adarga* a leather shield, *astillero* a rack for lances, *rocín* a poor horse, *hidalgo* "gentleman of the lower nobility", *aceña* (not glossed), *romance* "the vernacular, Spanish" (glosses of the course; the DLE is to be consulted).
3. The statement that the days of the week take the plural article for the habitual meaning (*los sábados*) and the singular for one day: the course's teaching, not checked against the Manual.
4. *duelos y quebrantos* "disputed" (no source named; the Archive lists a monograph of 1907, *Duelos y quebrantos (I, cap. 1)*, that argues the point).
5. The reading of *ser persona* as "a person in the full sense", and of *siete* as the Seven Sages (the English of Jacobs takes it so; the Spanish does not say so).
6. *en su punto* "at the point at which a thing is as it should be" (the course's gloss; the example *El arroz está en su punto* is the course's own).
7. The claim that the subject follows the verb in *Era mi padre aficionado* because the clause introduces a new person is the course's own, and rests on the order deck (`lang.v.es/order`).
8. The comparison with the First Folio of Shakespeare ("vp", "Iulius Cæsar") is from general knowledge, not from a page image. **Checked in B10 against a page image of the First Folio and corrected** (the Folio heading is "IVLIVS CÆSAR" in capitals; the first page has "vpon"); see `B10-FACT-CHECK-OCT-26.md`.
9. The editors: Aribau (died 1862), Chaytor (1871-1954), Mesa y López (dates unknown), Reyes (1889-1959). The course quotes modernized texts of the works of authors who died before 1946; the editors of Chaytor, Mesa y López, and Reyes may hold rights in their editions in some countries (the editions are of 1922, about 1912, and 1918, so they are in the public domain in the United States).
10. The question points use the course's own fresh Spanish sentences (*la ciudad cuyas calles son estrechas*, *Los de mi calle son simpáticos*, *Sepa usted que el tren ya salió*, *A ti te llaman Pepe*, *Llegó tarde, por lo cual perdió el tren*, *Si yo lo supiera, te lo dijera*, *El tener amigos fieles es un bien*, *Prometo ser honesta*, *El examen fue más difícil de lo que esperábamos*, *Los vecinos con quienes hablé ayer*, *Voy aprendiendo el oficio poco a poco*, *El arroz está en su punto*, *Más se gasta hoy que antes*, *El incendio destruyó todo un barrio*) and one of the Manual (*Mucho tiempo ha que no nos vemos*, §24.2.4d), checked against the rules above and not against a native reader.
11. In the Teresa passage the third sentence was left out because the edition prints *comenzó* (singular) after *Estos* (plural), a reading that is in the autograph tradition (De la Fuente, BAE 53, note 4: "En el original y en la edición de Salamanca dice *Estos*; en las demás ediciones *Esto*").
