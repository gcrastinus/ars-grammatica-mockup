Timothy's rule for B5 (26 Oct 2026): the only sources for word origin are the Manual and the DLE. Lapesa was not available. Every etymology below is checked in the DLE entry named; none comes from memory. Greek forms are not printed in the course, because the OCR garbles Greek letters. ## Resting on one source or on an inference

1. Every etymology rests on the DLE alone, read in an OCR copy of the 2014 printing; the Manual says little about origin (it states the Latin origin of *-mente*, §7.6.1, and the notion of learned bases). The live DLE should be consulted in B14.
2. The Arabic-article claim of the Phase 1 overview (`lang.prelim.es/overview`) that many Spanish words begin with the Arabic article *al-* is not in the Manual or in the DLE entries read here; it rests on no source in the course. It stays in the B14 row.
3. "Arabic of Spain" for the abbreviation *ár. hisp.*.
4. "Reconstructed" for the starred Germanic and Gothic forms.
5. The rule on the number of the noun in V+N compounds (summary of the Manual's lists).
6. *Enlatar* as an example of the pattern *en-N-ar*.
7. The Latin etyma are printed without macrons, as the DLE gives them; style rule 6 on quantity is not followed there.
8. The formation exercise asks about common bases (*conducir*, *crecer*, *dormir*, *elegir*, *casa*, *comer*, *mano*, *vender*, *fácil*) that earlier decks print as examples of other things. The property asked is new (the noun that the base gives), but the word is not.
