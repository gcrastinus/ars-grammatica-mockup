# Spanish, batch B10: fact check of `lang.ix.es/prose`, passages 6 to 10 (26 Oct 2026)

Scope: the five new passages of the deck (Fernán Caballero, *La Gaviota* I; Bécquer, "Los ojos verdes"; Alarcón, *El sombrero de tres picos* I; Galdós, *Marianela* I; Unamuno, *Del sentimiento trágico de la vida* I), the 20 note panels, the five new question points, and the one change to the card on spelling (the comparison with the First Folio). MANUAL is the Academy's *Nueva gramática de la lengua española: Manual* (2010), read in the machine-read text of the Internet Archive (item RAEManualDeLaNuevaGramaticaDeLaLenguaEspanola), not compared with the printed book (B14). Excerpts are saved in `rae-manual-2010-excerpts-B10.txt`. DPD is the *Diccionario panhispánico de dudas* (2005), read in the Internet Archive copies of its entries (`dpd-2005-excerpts-B10.txt`).

## Passages replaced

The plan listed Larra, "Vuelva usted mañana" (passage 6) and Clarín, *La Regenta* ch. 1 (passage 8). No public-domain English translation of either was found: a web search for an English "Vuelva usted mañana" found only an undated, unattributed English text on a web forum, and the Archive's catalog has none; *La Regenta* was first translated into English by John Rutherford in 1984 (Wikipedia; the Penguin catalog), which is under copyright. The rule is that prose never takes the course's own rendering, so both were dropped and replaced by passages that have a published translation in the public domain: Fernán Caballero, *La Gaviota* (English of J. Leander Starr, 1864) and Alarcón, *El sombrero de tres picos* (English of Jacob S. Fassett, Jr., 1918). The order of the deck is now chronological: Caballero (1849), Bécquer (1861), Alarcón (1874), Galdós (1878), Unamuno (1912). The plan names Serrano (1892) for *Marianela*; the English found is that of Clara Bell (1883), which is the one used (Mary J. Serrano's *Marianela* was not found).

## The comparison with the First Folio (B9 follow-up)

The B9 card said that the Folio has "vp" and "Iulius Cæsar". It was checked against a page image of the First Folio of 1623 (Internet Archive item mrvvilliamshakes00shak, leaf 737, signature page 109): the heading of the play is printed **"THE TRAGEDIE OF IVLIVS CÆSAR."** in capitals (V for U, I for J, and the ligature Æ), and the first page has "vpon" ("as euer trod vpon Neats Leather", "vp-on my handy-worke") and "vp" ("climb'd vp to Walles") with long s. So "Iulius Cæsar" in mixed case was not what the page shows. The card now quotes "vpon" and the heading "IVLIVS CÆSAR". The sentence on the passages "all older than 1800" now says "the first five passages".

## Points that rest on one source, on an inference, or on the course's own judgment (for B14)

1. The dates and places of first publication in the context panels: *La Gaviota* (*El Heraldo*, 1849; book of 1856), "Los ojos verdes" (*El Contemporáneo*, 15 December 1861), *El sombrero de tres picos* (1874), *Marianela* (1878), Unamuno (1912; some catalogues give 1913). The first two rest on web searches; the editions' own title pages do not give them.
2. The Spanish editions: the Appleton *Sombrero* and the Renacimiento Unamuno have no date on the title page (the Archive's catalog gives 1874 and 1912 for them, and the Appleton record is probably wrong); the *Marianela* edition is dated 1888 by the Archive's record and by the machine reading of the title page. None of the five Spanish texts was compared with another copy.
3. The English texts: Fassett (all three sentences), Bates and Bates (both sentences), and Flitch (all four sentences) were compared with the page images; Starr's first sentence was compared with the page image and his second sentence was read in Project Gutenberg's proofread text (#48698); Bell's first four lines were compared with the page image and the rest of the paragraph was read in Project Gutenberg's text (#48818). Bell's printing seems to have no full stop after "died gently away" (the scan shows a gap); the course prints the full stop of the Gutenberg text. The dash before "The reader perceives" is printed "--" in the Gutenberg text, and the course prints it as a dash. B14 should compare the unread parts with the page images.
4. The glosses of the course: *paquete de vapor* "a steamboat that carries mail and passengers on a regular line", *cuartilla* "a sheet of writing paper", *á capricho* "as the fancy takes it", *ir de vencida* "near its end" (taken from the English "drawing to a close"), *guinderos* "wild cherry trees" (Bell's rendering), *consta* "it is on record", *brazos* the paddles of the steamer (Starr's rendering); none was checked in the DLE.
5. The reading of *pues* in *Reinaba, pues, todavía* as drawing a consequence from the date; the reading of *el de la noche* as *el paso de la noche*; the reading of *soñolienta* as a metaphor; the reading of *tenia* in "Hace mucho tiempo que tenia ganas" as a wish seen in its course; the reading of *hoy … he puesto* as belonging to a period that includes the present: the course's own.
6. *Vino … la noche*, *murieron … los últimos rumores*, *Reinaba … Don Carlos*: the statement that the subject follows verbs of this kind in sentences that present something extends the Manual's statement on bare noun groups (§15.6.2b) to definite subjects and to the order deck (`lang.v.es/order`).
7. The statement that the object *a ningún otro hombre* has no doubling pronoun is an inference from §16.6.1e (indefinite pronouns) and from the Manual's *No vieron a nadie*.
8. The capital letters of *Siglo* and *Jefe* are described as capitals that give the nouns weight; today's lowercase is stated without a source.
9. The line of Terence (*Heautontimorumenos*) and the statement that Unamuno writes *homo sum; nihil humani a me alienum puto*: the course's knowledge, not checked in a text of Terence. The note on the Latin gives *nihil humani* as a genitive of the whole (A&G §346) and *alienum* as agreeing with *nihil* and, in Unamuno's own line, with *hominem*.
10. The edition of *Marianela* prints *qué avanzaba* with an accent; the school editions of 1903 print *que* (read in the text layer, not in the page image). The pen marks in the scanned copy (over *vereda* and *hombres*) were read as marks and not as letters, by comparison with the English.
11. The comparison of the spelling of *aun mas* (1856) with the DPD of 2005 uses the later rule on *aún*; the rule of 1856 was not consulted.
12. The question points use the course's own fresh Spanish sentences (*El tren se alejaba de la estación*, *Salió de la casa cantando*, *La torre era más alta que el castillo*, *Hace una hora que espero*, *Esta semana he escrito tres cartas*, *Se me cayó el vaso*, *Este coche es más rápido que el de mi hermano*, *No se sabe el año*, *El viajero caminaba apresurando el paso*, *A medida que subíamos, hacía más frío*, *Lo útil no siempre es lo bello*, *No quiero té sino café*, *Yo diría que no es posible*), checked against the rules above and not against a native reader.
13. The idea of the plan that Don Quijote I.8 may be replaced by the *Conde Lucanor* exemplo 35 in B14 (Timothy, 26 Oct 2026) is recorded in the B14 row; the translation (James York, 1868) is not verified.
