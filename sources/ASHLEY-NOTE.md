# Benedict Ashley, O.P., *The Arts of Learning and Communication* (1958)

A secondary resource only. Use the two passages below; ignore the rest of the book for this app. Do not use his etymologies (several are folk etymologies, e.g., *religio* from *religo*, p. 424).

1. **III.2 The Reasons for the Parts:** pp. 44–49. He derives the parts of speech from the categories and tests each word with "What does it modify? … What category does it express?" (p. 47). This is a useful pattern for question points. Present it as one later (Modist/Thomist) way of grounding the parts, not as Donatus's or Priscian's own account.
2. **I.6 Ambiguity / analogy:** pp. 49–51 and 424–426. Graded examples of univocal, equivocal, and analogical words ("bark," "sound," "stone," "light"). Good examples for cards.
