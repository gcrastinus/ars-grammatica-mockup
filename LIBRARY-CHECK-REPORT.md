## Needing Timothy's judgment

1. **Lowth and the preposition rule (vi.usage / prescribe-describe).** The card says that some eighteenth-century rules, "such as the rule against ending a sentence with a preposition, were taken from Latin rather than from English usage," and names Lowth as an example. Lowth himself allowed the final preposition in familiar style and only preferred the other order in the solemn style. This is from memory, because his text could not be reached. The card does not say that Lowth made the rule, but it may suggest it. Should the sentence be qualified?
2. **Port-Royal *Logique* chapter numbers.** The chapters moved between editions; in the 1685 English translation they are I.7 and II.4. Which edition should the course cite? The card text gives no chapter, so this concerns only the source notes and the sources line.
3. **Priscian's term (iii.figure / species).** Priscian's word for the noun's first species is *principalis*. The card keeps the English "primitive," which is the usual English term and is Priscian's own word for the verb's species. Is that acceptable, or should the card say "principal (primitive)"?
4. **The edition of *Rasselas* (v.clause).** The cards print the sentence as Morley's edition (1889) prints it: "everywhere," and no comma before "and." The 1759 text, which may read "every where" and have the comma, could not be reached. Which edition should be the course's copy text?
5. **Varro's section numbers (iii.verb-2 / aspect-latin, source note).** IX.96–97 and X.47–48 remain unconfirmed. The doctrine and Varro's examples are verified. Keep the numbers or cite only the books?
6. **Network access.** To finish the unreachable items, these sites would need to be allowed for the session: archive.org (Keil *GL* 3, Daiches, Bacon, Lowth, the Port-Royal *Grammaire*), Corpus Thomisticum, Wikisource, Gutenberg, and OAPEN (Tesnière, *De copia*).
