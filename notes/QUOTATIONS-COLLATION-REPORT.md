# Quotation collation, 21 Oct 2026

The quotations named in section 7 of notes/LANGUAGES-FINISH-REPORT.md were read against an edition. A quotation matches when the words, accents, breathings, and internal punctuation agree. A period, comma, or Greek high stop that only closes an excerpt, or that belongs to a sentence the course does not finish, is not counted as a difference. Macrons, and the diaeresis in *aëra*, were left wherever the letters agreed. French spacing before ! and the curly apostrophe, where an edition has a straight apostrophe and no space, were left as the course's house style.

The Perseus hopper did not serve the Greek. The Greek was read in the Perseus texts of the PerseusDL canonical-greekLit repository, which are the texts the hopper uses. Servius was read in the Wikisource transcription of Thilo's text, the same text as Perseus 1999.02.0053. That file is a transcription, not a photograph of the printed page.

## Servius

All eight notes, and two further quotations in the Servius deck, match the Wikisource transcription of Thilo, *In Vergilii Aeneidem commentarii*, Liber I. https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/In_Vergilii_Aeneidem_commentarii/Liber_I. This is a transcription, not the printed Teubner page.

- *arma* (I.1): *per ‘arma’ autem bellum significat, et est tropus metonymia*. Matches.
- *multum ille* (I.3): *‘multille’ conlisio est*. Matches. The period is omitted. The spelling *conlisio* agrees.
- *ille* (I.3): *‘ille’ hoc loco abundat … propter metri necessitatem*. The words that are printed match. The ellipsis skips *est enim interposita particula*. The full note continues *ut stet versus: nam si detrahas ‘ille’, stat sensus*, which is what the explanation says. The ellipsis was left.
- *multa quoque et bello passus* (I.5): *una vacat*. Matches. The note says *naturaliter nemo coniungit*, which is what the prompt paraphrases.
- *mihi* (I.8): *‘mihi’ longa hic, alibi brevis*. Matches.
- *volvere casus* (I.9): *id est casibus volvi. et est figura hypallage*. Matches.
- *hic illius arma* (I.16): *adverbium pro adverbio posuit, praesentis loci pro absentis*, and in the explanation *debuit enim dicere ‘illic’*. Matches. The edition joins those clauses with a semicolon. The course splits them between the prompt and the explanation.
- *duci* (I.19): *ductum iri*. Matches. The note continues *infinitivi modi a passivo tempus praesens pro futuro posuit*, which is what the explanation says.
- Deck preface, not one of the eight: *In exponendis auctoribus haec consideranda sunt: poetae vita, titulus operis, qualitas carminis, scribentis intentio, numerus librorum, ordo librorum, explanatio*. Matches.
- Deck, *Italiam* (I.2): *Italiam venit pro ad Italiam venit*. Matches the words of *dicens ‘Italiam venit’ pro ad Italiam venit*.

## Decisions for Timothy

1. *Rhetorica ad Herennium* IV.39, in `lang.vii.la/figures.js`, the card "Four schemes in the authors," and the third variant of question point `q-la-fig-4`. Marx prints *Esse oportet, ut vivas, non vivere, ut edis.* The course now follows him, with the macrons kept. The text it had was the proverb's form, *esse oportet ut vivas, non vivere ut edas*, with no commas and with the subjunctive. The English is the same on either reading. Recommendation: keep Marx. Restore *edās* and drop the commas if the proverb is preferred to the Teubner text.
2. Hesiod, *Works and Days* 40, in the Greek tropes exercise. The verse has *παντὸς* with a grave, because the sentence continues. The course quotes the proverb alone, with the acute *παντός*. Recommendation: keep the acute.
3. Corneille, *Le Cid* II.2, in `lang.vii.fr/tropes.js`. Marty-Laveaux prints *A vaincre*. The course has modern *À*. Recommendation: keep *À*.
4. Goethe, "Mignon," in `lang.vii.de/tropes.js`. Gutenberg 2337 prints *Kennst du das Land, wo die Zitronen blühn*, in Lehrjahre III.1. The 1795 title on German Wikisource has *Land?* and *Citronen*, and the page is not a full transcription. Recommendation: keep *Zitronen* and the comma.
5. Ronsard, *Sonnets pour Hélène* II.24, in the French tropes exercise. The same number is the title of the section X deck, which was not changed. Sorg (1921) numbers the sonnet XLII and prints *dés aujourd’huy*. Classroom editions use II.24 and the modern spelling. The Pléiade uses II.43. Recommendation: keep II.24 and *dès aujourd’hui*, so the exercise and the section X deck still agree.
6. Paul Gerhardt, "O Haupt voll Blut und Wunden" 1–2, in `lang.ii.de/meter-ex.js`. Still unread on Wikisource, Gutenberg, or Gallica. The words agree with EG 85 as reprinted on the German Wikipedia article, except the capital *Voll* at the start of the second line. Recommendation: leave both lines until a 1656 or 1660 text, or a Wikisource transcription, is read.
