## Panel 1: Conjunctions, possessive adjectives, appositives, and infinitives

A conjunction signifies by the mode of joining two things, and so it requires two words to join; it depends on both. In "Peter and Paul run," draw an arrow from "and" to "Peter" and from "and" to "Paul," and, since the running belongs to each of them, draw an arrow from "run" to "Peter" and from "run" to "Paul." So also in *Petrus et Paulus currunt*: *et* depends on *Petrus* and on *Paulus*, and *currunt* depends on each. Mark "or" in the same way: in "Tea or coffee pleases him," "or" depends on "Tea" and on "coffee." When the conjunction joins two clauses, it depends on the verb of each: in "The wind rose, and the ships sailed," "and" depends on "rose" and on "sailed."

A possessive adjective is an adjective, so it depends on its noun: in "My brother reads," "My" depends on "brother"; in *frāter meus legit*, *meus* depends on *frāter*.

A noun in apposition explains another noun, as an adjective does, and so it depends on that noun: in "Paul, the apostle, wrote," "apostle" depends on "Paul"; in *Cicerō cōnsul dīxit* ("Cicero, the consul, spoke"), *cōnsul* depends on *Cicerō*. An infinitive that completes a verb is marked as its object: in "She wants to sing," "wants" depends on "sing," and "to," which is not one of Donatus's eight parts, is drawn as depending on "sing," as the article depends on its noun. In *volō legere* ("I wish to read"), *volō* depends on *legere*.

## Panel 2: Relatives

A relative pronoun refers to a noun already named, its antecedent, and agrees with it in gender and number, as an adjective agrees with its noun; so the relative depends on its antecedent, and the relative clause hangs from the antecedent by the relative. Within its own clause the relative is marked like any noun. In "The boy who runs wins," "who" depends on "boy," "runs" depends on "who" as on its subject, and "wins" depends on "boy." In *puer quem vīdī currit* ("the boy whom I saw runs"), *quem* depends on *puer*, *vīdī* depends on *quem* as on its object, and *currit* depends on *puer*. Mark an adjective clause introduced by "that" or "which" in the same way.

Source line: Thomas of Erfurt, *Grammatica speculativa*, on construction, the pronoun, and the conjunction (Bursill-Hall), in paraphrase.
