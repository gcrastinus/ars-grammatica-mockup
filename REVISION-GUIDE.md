# Revision Guide: Timothy's wording changes and decisions (Ars Grammatica)

Read this before drafting any new section. Each pair is BEFORE (draft) → AFTER (Timothy's revision). Learn the pattern behind each change, not only the phrase. Newest entries are at the bottom; add to this file whenever he revises a card.

**How to use the pairs:** they show Timothy's taste in particular sentences. They are illustrations, not rules. Do not turn a single change (e.g., "treats" → "considers") into a blanket substitution. Only the numbered decisions in section A apply everywhere.

## A. Decisions (apply everywhere)

1. **"Fault"** only when reporting the ancients or translating *vitium*. Our own words: error, barbarism, solecism, impropriety. Section VI is "Correctness and Errors."
2. **"Office(s)"** only when translating *officium/officia* or reporting Varro, Diomedes, Quintilian. Our own words: "the tasks of grammar" (the art), "the tasks of the grammarian" (the person).
3. **Indirect questions are not banned,** just not to be used by habit. Mix direct questions, noun phrases, and indirect questions where they read naturally. Not everything has to be a question.
4. **No example lists at the bottom of panels.** Examples go in the text. When the text illustrates a kind (a noun, a case, a figure), give more than one example.
5. **Sources:** one Sources note on a deck's first panel, listing only the backbone sources of the doctrine. A text quoted in passing is cited by its usual short title and locus in the text and is not thereby a "source."
6. **No translator names in the text,** except for a long quoted passage or where the translation itself matters or is disputed (e.g., the translation section). Record them in sources/TRANSLATIONS-USED.md. Never credit "T. Kearns" unless he actually made the translation.
7. **No technical terms before they are taught.** No "syllogism" in early decks; say "argument," "demonstration," or "account."
8. **Do not reuse the Trivium introduction's wording or arguments.** The model passages in CLAUDE.md show style only. Where general reasons are needed, summarize briefly and link to the [Introduction to the Trivium](https://studiuminstitute.org/trivium/).
9. **Prefer the ancient sources' own account first,** then the general philosophical reasons.
10. **Avoid old-fashioned renderings in our own voice** ("fitting speech," "in a fashion servile"). Use plain modern English, and keep the Latin word visible where it matters.
